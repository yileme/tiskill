## PDF page 12
1.1–2.2 prefer accessible Irish/British English and avoid obscure colloquialisms; reproduce quotations/titles faithfully, marking editorial interventions. English punctuation spacing and heading/footnote stops apply, with truncation/contraction distinction.

## PDF page 13
Ellipsis marks omissions with specified spacing; omitted paragraphs use a bracketed ellipsis on its own line. This is quotation/editorial notation, not authority to omit translated substance. Colons introduce expansion after a complete clause; run-in heads take stops.

## PDF page 14
Semicolons separate independent clauses or complex list items. House style normally omits the serial comma but adds one to prevent ambiguity; coordinate adjectives take commas, cumulative ones do not.

## PDF page 15
Linked-clause and parenthetical commas depend on structure; paired insertions must leave a complete sentence when removed. Short time/place openings may run on; sentence adverbs normally take commas. Apply for sense, not mechanical source punctuation copying.

## PDF page 16
Subordinate-clause commas change scope: defining clauses restrict a proposition, nondefining ones add information. Cows and insurance-law examples show that punctuation can change factual claims; verify intended relation before editing.

## PDF page 17
Defining relatives may use that, which or who; nondefining relatives cannot use that. Restrictive apposition identifies a referent and lacks commas; supplementary apposition takes commas. Do not ban defining which as ungrammatical.

## PDF page 18
Short-form office titles precede names without commas; reduce comma congestion by restructuring. Spaced en dashes give emphasis but should be sparse and avoided in legislation; round brackets reduce emphasis and can nest.

## PDF page 19
Closed-up legislative citations and square-bracket editorial/translation explanations have distinct functions. Direct questions take question marks; indirect questions do not; polite instructions follow a special house convention. Exclamation is register-sensitive.

## PDF page 20
Factorial ! is mathematical notation. Curly single quotes nest double quotes; punctuation follows whether it belongs to the quotation, with an ISG exception for amendments. Long quotations use indentation without quote marks.

## PDF page 21
Retain actual English quotations, remove merely foreignness-marking quotes, and seek original wording before back-translation; otherwise report indirectly. Neutral foreign so-called equivalents may need no lexical rendering, but retain meaning; publication titles and appositional names follow distinct conventions.

## PDF page 22
Slash distinguishes a noncalendar twelve-month year from a multi-year range and has spacing rules for alternatives. Possessive nouns differ from pronouns; retain established spelling of business/place names rather than regularising blindly.

## PDF page 23
Singular names ending s generally take apostrophe-s; alternatives exist, with fixed institutional and for-sake exceptions. Contractions suit informal registers; plural abbreviations and numerals do not take apostrophes; lower-case letters may.

## PDF page 24
Abbreviation verbs can take apostrophes but fuller wording often reads better. British dictionary/spellchecker aids require judgement; -ise and -ize are BOTH correct, with -ise chosen for EU consistency. Preserve official foreign/international spellings; -yse guidance is within British usage.

## PDF page 25
Spelling rules distinguish digraph exceptions, consonant doubling and legal judgment from general judgement. Metre/meter and tonne/ton encode real distinctions; a/an historical allows variation. Preserve quoted official carcase spelling.

## PDF page 26
Input/output past forms and domain-specific plurals require contextual checks: book indexes versus scientific indices, political formulas versus scientific formulae, forums except ancient Rome. Several plural alternatives are expressly permitted.

## PDF page 27
British licence/license and practice/practise distinguish noun/verb; French interference can distort spelling. Compound form follows usage/dictionaries, and hyphens should clarify attachment rather than be applied indiscriminately.

## PDF page 28
Hyphens disambiguate little-used and re-cover; unpack stacked noun modifiers with prepositions. No hyphen after -ly adverbs or ever plus comparative, while well-known-type modifiers generally need one. Preserve semantic grouping.

## PDF page 29
Noun-participle modifiers are hyphenated; many compounds take hyphens only attributively, others also predicatively. Chemical open compounds are exempt; established EU prefix spellings can override dictionary defaults.

## PDF page 30
Phrasal verbs stay separate while noun/adjective forms vary; check established spellings. Hyphenate written numbers and attributive fractions; coordinate only genuinely hyphenated compounds, not truncated solid words.

## PDF page 31
Differentiate individual hyphenated surnames from joint names linked by en dash. Technical closed compounds may be precise terms; capitals generally mark proper names and should otherwise be restrained. Names must not be respelled mechanically.

## PDF page 32
Programmes/policies normally use lowercase even when an acronym exists. Full institution/unit/office titles take capitals; EU permanent bodies/functions have specific plural exceptions, while ad hoc delegations stay lowercase.

## PDF page 33
Foreign names retain original capitalisation; translated official titles follow English rules, descriptions lowercase. Full international agreement titles differ from short references. Informal ministry/government references need not mimic formal diplomatic naming.

## PDF page 34
Short-form capitals may prevent Funds/funds ambiguity. Specific EU acts and numbered Annex/Article references take capitals, generic/proposed acts do not. Publication typography distinguishes full works from articles and long titles.

## PDF page 35
Sentence case governs document headings. Named periods/events/days differ from seasons and budget years; numbered Figure/Table/Chapter differ from lowercase page/paragraph/point. Political organisation names differ from ideological adjectives.

## PDF page 36
State is generally lowercase, but treaty-rooted Member States, State aid and Heads of State or Government are exceptions. Geographic proper names differ from directional descriptions; NUTS names follow their separate authority-based convention.

## PDF page 37
Genericised trademarks lose capitals; celestial Earth differs from everyday earth. Proper-name derivatives require dictionary judgement; title-case hyphen compounds allow two patterns rather than one mandatory universal form.

## PDF page 38
Respect organisations’ own capitalisation, including Anti-Fraud and Co-operation; one-letter technical prefixes can remain lowercase initially. Avoid block capitals for emphasis; full-sentence quotations and EU signatory surnames have specific conventions.

## PDF page 39
Preserve personal-name accents and preferred spellings; Ms/Mx depend on known preference and identity, with no title preferable when unknown. Retain source Dr except combined titles; use official English body names and verified ministerial titles.

## PDF page 40
In legally binding English, use official body names and transliterate non-Latin names. Elsewhere translate descriptive ministries with optional original, but retain proper company names; explanatory brackets and shortened later mentions must preserve identification.

## PDF page 41
Do not invent English acronyms for foreign bodies; introduce original abbreviations. Recover underlying Latin names instead of back-transliterating mechanically. Vehicle names are italic, pronouns register-sensitive; native geographic names are default except established English forms.

## PDF page 42
Country/capital/currency forms refer to ISG A5; distinguish Gaza territory/city and Mexico country/capital. Nordic precisely covers Nordic Council countries; Scandinavian varies geographically and linguistically. Administrative regions need established translations.

## PDF page 43
Traditional regions, development areas, rivers, seas, lakes and islands follow distinct naming rules. Greenland waters implies jurisdiction, unlike waters off Greenland; geographic normalisation must retain such substantive distinctions.

## PDF page 44
Valley translation depends on ordinary geography versus official/product names; compass capitals reflect named entities. Postal addresses stay original except translated uppercase country, with non-Latin transliteration; do not translate streets or postal-box labels.

## PDF page 45
Numbers one–nine generally use words, including time units; figures apply to web content, decimals, comparisons, votes, ranges, serials and measurement. Names/titles and quotations can override defaults; do not impose the opening rule without exceptions.

## PDF page 46
Do not mix figures with hundred/thousand; million/billion/trillion can combine except OJ monetary amounts, which require full figures. Adjacent figures may need words for readability; ordinal/legal date conventions depend on genre.

## PDF page 47
Written financial amounts preserve numeric grouping and and. Prefer rewording figure-led sentences, with percentage exception; hard-space thousands have public/web exceptions. Billion means 10^9, money m/bn differ from forbidden shortcuts; retain serial system.

## PDF page 48
Latin, Greek and Bulgarian serial labels require positional conversion, not letter transliteration; EU continuation is aa…zz then aaa…zzz. Fractions distinguish adjectival/adverbial hyphens from noun phrases; decimal separator rule continues next page.

## PDF page 49
OJ production replaces decimal points with commas and adds percent-symbol spacing; these publication exceptions differ from authoring guidance. Preserve precision and percentage-point versus relative-percent changes; compressed ranges and full dates follow separate rules.

## PDF page 50
Information-only translations may retain consistent date conventions. Decades lack apostrophes; chronology labels have fixed positions. Legislative year spans use to; slash denotes one noncalendar year. Omit redundant year only when absolutely clear.

## PDF page 51
After excludes its named date; by sets a deadline while until describes duration. Entry into force, effect and cessation wording must retain legal timing. Put date qualifiers first except when reproducing an actual title.

## PDF page 52
24-hour times use leading zeros/colon; 12-hour forms use point and a.m./p.m., with noon/midnight clearer than 12 labels. h marks duration, not clock time. Explain unfamiliar abbreviations and avoid those used rarely.

## PDF page 53
Short forms can replace acronym repetition; never improvise translated abbreviations. Acronyms versus initialisms differ in pronunciation and typography; length-based capital rules have official-name and conventional exceptions.

## PDF page 54
Initialisms usually take capitals without stops but mixed forms exist. e.g./i.e. remain lowercase with stops; truncations take stops, symbols/codes do not. Personal initials normally preserve accents and compound-name structure.

## PDF page 55
Digraph and transliterated initials may span two letters; respect known name preferences. Articles depend on spoken sounds and established usage. Abbreviation plurals normally add s; invariant plural exceptions must avoid ambiguity.

## PDF page 56
Foreign abbreviations retain original case; e.g./i.e. take preceding punctuation but no following comma in this style. Mathematical expressions remain grammatical sentence parts; use genuine minus/multiplication glyphs for accessibility and preserve negative-value spacing.

## PDF page 57
Mathematical symbols vary by language: division glyph can mean subtraction or range elsewhere. Convert by meaning, not appearance; ± denotes tolerance, not vague approximation. Unit names lowercase and inflect normally despite capitalised personal-name derivatives.

## PDF page 58
Unit symbols have exact case, no plural s/stops and hard-space separation. Do not automatically convert non-SI quantities; explanations may be footnoted. Distinguish temperature/angle spacing, computing binary usage and kW capacity versus kWh energy.

## PDF page 59
Use ISO currency code before amount, compulsory in legal acts; symbols suit public/web products. Negative sign precedes currency; table scale is explicit. Euro/cent plurals are prescribed house/legal conventions, not proof other English plurals are invalid.

## PDF page 60
Foreign phrases need accents and often italics, but assimilated words and proper names differ; verbatim foreign quotations use quotes in roman type. Prefer English to unnecessary Latin while checking equivalence; Greek romanisation defaults to ELOT with established exceptions.

## PDF page 61
Section 9.5 says to follow the annex and omit hard/soft signs (but the language-specific annex on PDF 131 conflicts with that blanket instruction); Arabic needs English-specific spelling. Chinese defaults to pinyin but Hohhot, Kowloon, Kaohsiung and Mitsubishi show why conventional identity outranks mechanical transliteration.

## PDF page 62
Adjective ordering usually moves opinion before factual size/age/shape/colour/origin/material. Clarify biannual/biennial when uncertain. Some pronominal adverbs remain normal or legally useful; their presence alone does not establish bad English.

## PDF page 63
Replace archaic pronominal adverbs where appropriate, preserving hereinafter/hereafter and therefor/therefore meanings. Hence/ thus express consequence rather than manner or simultaneity. Position only near its semantic focus for precision.

## PDF page 64
Only can restrict action, object or agent; placement changes meaning. British in future means from now on, unlike in the future; US usage differs. Collective agreement follows sense, money allows either, fractions agree with countability.

## PDF page 65
Decimal/zero countables take plural; plural-looking country names take singular. Data and media permit contextual variation, -ics depends on meaning. Neither/nor and none agreement rules do not justify a blanket singular-only rule for all quantifiers.

## PDF page 66
One in X can use notional plural; combined activities can form a singular whole. People is default but persons remains valid for legal entities, official titles and restrictions. Perfect versus simple past depends on connection to present.

## PDF page 67
Minutes use reported-speech tense/time shifts, with simplification to avoid repeated pluperfects and speech tags when attribution stays clear. Modal shifts need judgement. Legal verb guidance is introduced as limited rather than exhaustive.

## PDF page 68
10.26–31 explicitly limits rules to MAIN clauses of EU enacting terms: shall obligation, shall not prohibition, may permission, need not permission to omit, present for direct declarations. Non-EU legislation may consistently use must; may not is ambiguous for prohibition.

## PDF page 69
Future/contingent declarations can use shall; actual amendments use present. Added means at end, inserted elsewhere. 10.33 excludes shall from recitals, subordinate clauses and indirect legislative quotation/paraphrase; choose must/required to as appropriate.

## PDF page 70
Annex commands use imperative and objective necessity must; may not still risks ambiguity. Split infinitives are not ungrammatical; avoid moving modifiers into ambiguity. Object/genitive subjects of -ing forms both exist, with context-dependent meanings.

## PDF page 71
Rephrase -ing clauses to make causal/conditional meaning explicit. Correlative conjunctions need parallel structure; not only…but also should convey genuine added emphasis. Lists retain original numbering type and grammatical continuation, with automatic-numbering exceptions.

## PDF page 72
Four list patterns differ by item grammar and sentence count: short items, completed lead-ins, independent statements and multi-sentence entries. Capitalisation/colon/semicolon rules are house-specific; web lists omit intermediate comma/semicolon punctuation.

## PDF page 73
Legal-reference formulas depend on what a provision does: requirement, objective, empowerment, argument or definition. Under is broad fallback; do not copy shall into a paraphrase. The table offers choices, not single-word equivalence for every selon.

## PDF page 74
Insert real footnotes before punctuation; printers add brackets, so do not manually simulate them. Nonpublication source conventions can remain. Bibliographical authoring follows ISG, translation follows source; use official English titles but do not invent translated titles.

## PDF page 75
COM/SWD preparatory titles use roman type; running text may quote titles. Cross-reference sections by their actual labels, using § only if present in the target. Incoming-letter simplification is explicitly a correspondence workflow, not general omission authority.

## PDF page 76
Noneditable incoming-letter template excludes salutations; outgoing letters pair known-name sincerely and generic faithfully consistently. Exchange-of-letters agreements retain diplomatic formulas and the acceptance text that constitutes agreement.

## PDF page 77
Notes verbales have set third-person formulas. Inclusive-language guidance primarily addresses publication; information-only translations must convey author intention. Gender-neutral occupational terms are preferred, but historical legislation may retain generic masculine language.

## PDF page 78
Prefer neutral alternatives to generic man terms, with technical man-made fibres exception. Use known individual pronouns; otherwise second person, imperative or plural can avoid unnecessary gender marking while preserving reference.

## PDF page 79
Singular they is accepted; omit/recast pronouns only when sense survives. Person-first disability wording is generally preferred but disabled people also accepted; neutral expressions avoid stigmatizing assumptions. Relationship/community labels depend on context.

## PDF page 80
Respect current names/pronouns and neutral age/name terminology. Biological taxonomy requires capital genus, lowercase species and italics, with roman rank markers and cultivar quotes. Higher-rank typography has explicit nomenclature/legal exceptions, not a universal italic rule.

## PDF page 81
Taxonomic author citations stay roman; serovar is unabbreviated. Virus scientific and common names differ; first full genus may later abbreviate. Vernacular plants and wine grape names differ from botanical cultivars; geology capitals distinguish named units from generic labels.

## PDF page 82
Chemical formulas are interlingual; sulfur versus tariff sulphur is domain-sensitive. Chemical hyphenation overrides everyday compound rules; numerical locants need exact punctuation. Use authoritative inventory conventions and preserve source choice of common versus scientific name.

## PDF page 83
Physical page 83 contains only running header/page number; no substantive body. Included in coverage without attributing a fabricated rule.

## PDF page 84
Part II divider signals shift to EU-specific institutions and terminology. No substantive rule on this page; following recommendations must retain their institutional scope.

## PDF page 85
EU is not synonymous with Europe; Union shorthand belongs chiefly to treaties/legislation. Retain Community historically and Euratom currently; common and single/internal market require context. EU-27 needs temporal disambiguation; Brexit referendum differs from actual 2020 withdrawal.

## PDF page 86
Acquis includes primary/secondary law, case-law, external agreements and soft law; public-facing paraphrase must not narrow it to statutes alone. Primary legislation encompasses treaties and equivalent agreements; founding Communities differ from the later EU.

## PDF page 87
Treaties list historically, with current consolidated versions on EUR-Lex. TFEU is renamed EC/EEC Treaty, Euratom remains separate; Treaties of Rome means both. Merger and Budget treaty signing dates differ from entry-into-force dates.

## PDF page 88
Greenland withdrawal, Single European Act, Maastricht and Amsterdam have distinct legal effects and dates. Amsterdam renumbered treaty articles, so historical citations need version awareness; TEU abbreviation is not a generic label for all treaties.

## PDF page 89
Nice changed institutional arrangements and OJ title; Lisbon gave EU legal personality and renamed core treaty/institutions. Seven accession treaties precede actual accession years; retain historical names when citing contemporary instruments.

## PDF page 90
Treaty of Accession sets principles/ratification, Act contains technical transitional amendments. Legislation generally requires full treaty titles; commentary can use TEU/TFEU. Numbered subdivisions close up, unnumbered paragraphs use ordinal descriptive citation.

## PDF page 91
Regulations, directives and decisions differ from nonbinding opinions/recommendations; only directives are transposed, all can be implemented. Delegated/implementing titles and legislative/nonlegislative status are distinct. Ordinary procedure means Council/Parliament joint adoption.

## PDF page 92
Special procedures can involve consultation or consent. Draft proposal becomes proposal after Commission approval; accompanying memoranda differ. Post-2015 numbering uses domain/year/serial, publication year and common sequence; earlier year formats are not retroactively normalised.

## PDF page 93
Numbering exceptions include unnumbered agreements/corrigenda, dual-numbered ECB/PSC instruments and bracketed domainless publication numbers for agreement/EEA/EFTA/UNECE acts. Preserve both identifying systems rather than simplify away a number.

## PDF page 94
Historical regulation/directive/decision numbering varies by era; pre-2015 decision publication number differs from integral title number. Modern standardisation does not authorise rewriting historic citations; adoption and publication years may differ.

## PDF page 95
Joint acts require of the European Parliament and of the Council. ECSC and pre-Lisbon framework decisions/actions/positions retain historical citation forms; unpublished decisions use date. Multiple references have OJ-specific domain repetition rules.

## PDF page 96
OJ repeats domain abbreviation for every act while other text may group; chronological citation preferred. Amendment titles omit repeated institution/date under stated conditions. Preambles identify institution in capitals then legal/procedural citations beginning Having regard to.

## PDF page 97
Recitals follow Whereas and bracketed numbering with special final comma; full first act citations carry OJ footnotes. Enacting formula differs from internal decisions. Inserted EU Article 198a is a distinct article, not a subdivision; national rules differ.

## PDF page 98
Regulations, directives and decisions have different closing provisions, and force/application/transposition are not interchangeable. Commission enactment location versus draft Council blank and uppercase signatories are formal rules. French alinéa translates according to numbered-parent hierarchy.

## PDF page 99
Commission can mean College or whole institution; clarify ambiguity. Commissioner suits journalism but not legal acts; cabinet has a defined institutional meaning. DG abbreviations should not assume public familiarity.

## PDF page 100
Named Legal/Internal Audit Services differ from generic Commission services. Council General Secretariat is not Secretariat-General; meeting dates distinguish multi-day from overnight sessions. Council configurations and informal meetings have different purposes.

## PDF page 101
Council rotating Presidency differs from European Council President; Council of Europe is separate. Parliament normally has no article in short reference. Session, part-session and sitting are distinct; President’s Office is not automatically cabinet.

## PDF page 102
MEP, committee names and written-answer formula have prescribed uses. CJEU is an umbrella institution including Court of Justice and General Court; dissolved Civil Service Tribunal remains historically relevant. Jurisdiction and appeal descriptions require institution-specific naming.

## PDF page 103
ECLI uniquely identifies judgments/opinions using jurisdiction/court/year/serial components. Body references name decision type and court; parties italicised but v roman. Court publications have their own harmonised citation rules, distinct from general EU documents.

## PDF page 104
Footnotes order decision type/court/date/name/number/ECLI/pinpoint. External translations should retain unambiguous identification. ECR is historical and pre-1969 English pagination needs checking. CJEU, ECtHR and ICJ must never be conflated.

## PDF page 105
Multilingual Court/General Court names map to distinct bodies, even when superficially similar. ECA reports/opinions and Secretariat-General differ from judicial courts; EESC European title is acceptable despite treaty wording and distinct from UN ECOSOC.

## PDF page 106
Committee of the Regions preparatory Commissions differ from European Commission. ECB price-stability/monetary role differs from EIB lending and EIF SME risk/guarantee role; preserve institution and governance terminology rather than generalising to EU bank.

## PDF page 107
Interinstitutional bodies serve security, recruitment, training and publishing; agency participation may extend beyond EU states. OJ L/C/S series serve different purposes, electronic edition authentic since July 2013; full OJ title italicised in legislation.

## PDF page 108
Historical OJ title changes after January 2003. Since October 2023 each act has its own OJ reference and ELI format; old references retain initial page only, not page ranges. C-series ELI is optional but recommended; short Official Journal stays roman.

## PDF page 109
General Report references changed from ordinal to year-based in 1994; Bulletin is a historical publication. Own resources include customs/VAT/GNI/plastics; proposed new resources must not be described as adopted. MFF sets multi-year ceilings, distinct from annual budget.

## PDF page 110
NextGenerationEU recovery funding and RRF plans/grants/loans are distinct from ordinary annual budget. Budget hierarchy and budgetary authority are formal terms; direct/shared/indirect management identify who administers funding, not interchangeable synonyms.

## PDF page 111
Payment and commitment appropriations differ, as do differentiated/aggregate appropriations. Cancellation, carryover, decommitment and restored availability have distinct accounting meanings. Headings 1–2 organise innovation and cohesion programmes; retain official programme identities.

## PDF page 112
Headings 3–7 cover natural resources, migration, security, external action and administration with distinct funds/instruments. EAGF/EAFRD and EMFAF/old EMFF must not collapse; EIF and EDF are separate funds. Policy/date facts are source claims, not independently verified current law.

## PDF page 113
EDF’s pre-2021 outside-budget status differs from integration under 2021–27. Alphabetical member lists are reordered for English, protocol lists retained. Short country names generally suffice; Permanent Representation is an office, Representative a person.

## PDF page 114
Explain Coreper for public audiences and distinguish deputies/representatives. Explain national parliament names; translate party names where meaningful but retain national acronyms. National legal terminology follows reliable official translations; inserted articles need traceability.

## PDF page 115
National bis/ter and source-letter omissions remain unless official English says otherwise, with Greek/Bulgarian conversion exceptions. Legislative titles must identify acts unambiguously via number/date/official abbreviation, or original title when those identifiers are absent.

## PDF page 116
Concise common-law-style titles, descriptive source-shaped titles and hybrids are all acceptable depending on clarity. Restructure to reduce cumbersome syntax without obscuring instrument identity or pretending a national act is an English statute.

## PDF page 117
Title choices must be consistent/readable; act versus law is contextual, not absolute, and draft act/law preferred subject to official usage. National gazette names follow country practice. Language ordering/codes have legislative versus general-text exceptions.

## PDF page 118
Official/working languages are not legally distinguished as internal procedural languages are. External relations differs from CFSP foreign policy; EEA is not EFTA. Acceding/candidate/potential candidate have distinct statuses; avoid ambiguous accession country.

## PDF page 119
Third country may mean non-EU or nonparty, so explain for public readers. Preserve geographic contrasts even when Middle East is default; official organisation spellings override house style. US names/demonyms and religious labels require contextual accuracy; Kosovo footnote preserves status qualification.

## PDF page 120
