# DGT English Style Guide — complete close-reading synthesis

Source: European Commission, English Style Guide, eighth edition (January 2016), updated November 2025; running date 21 November 2025. Read all 138 physical PDF pages, including preliminary matter, blanks/dividers and all seven annexes. Printed pages 1–132 correspond to PDF 7–138. The ledger contains one substantive or structural note per physical page. This was sequential full-text consumption, not keyword selection or coverage inferred from hashes.

## Recommendations for downstream use

1. **Make clarity audience-dependent without weakening meaning.** The introduction (PDF 9–10) supports straightforward syntax, short paragraphs and usable lists for public readers. It explicitly accepts necessary technical/legal terminology and specialist shorthand for expert audiences. Explain acquis and third country for the public (PDF 86, 119); do not erase their scope. Unpack noun stacks (PDF 28), position only accurately (63–64), preserve causal/conditional relations (63, 71), and use genuinely parallel lists.
2. **Separate correctness from house style.** §3.2 (PDF 24) explicitly says both -ise and -ize are correct; -ise provides EU consistency. The British base does not invalidate other English varieties. Split infinitives are grammatical (70), data permits singular/plural (65), defining which is valid (17), and singular they is accepted (79). Punctuation, footnote placement, date/number typography and euro plurals are context-specific editorial choices.
3. **Scope legal shall exactly.** §§10.26–31 (PDF 68–69) concern main clauses in EU enacting terms: obligation shall, prohibition shall not, permission may, permission not to act need not; present tense for definitions/direct implementation. Future/contingent provisions may use shall. Recitals, subordinate clauses, indirect quotations/paraphrases and ordinary annex text use suitable alternatives (§10.33); annex commands use imperatives and objective necessity must (§10.34, PDF 70). Consistent must is permitted for non-EU legislation. Avoid ambiguous may not for prohibitions. Neither globally replace shall nor spread it across all legal prose.
4. **Preserve translation content and attribution.** Reproduce direct quotations/titles faithfully (§1.2, PDF 12). Ellipses explain how to mark quotation omissions, not when to delete translated substance (§2.3, 13). Seek original English quotations; if unavailable, report indirectly rather than fabricate verbatim wording (§2.35, 21). Removal of foreignness-marking quotation marks and neutral so-called equivalents is contextual. Incoming-letter header/salutation simplification (§14.1, 75–76) is a particular workflow. Pronoun omission in inclusive-language examples (79) removes redundant grammatical material, not propositions. None licenses unrequested abridgement of ordinary translations.
5. **Treat names and identifiers as evidence-bearing content.** Official English names and organisation spellings take priority (§3.3; §§5.8–14; Annex 1). Legally binding acts require official body names; descriptive ministry translations elsewhere can be explained. Retain company identity, preferred personal spellings, original abbreviations, diacritics and address conventions. Romanisation must use the correct language and exceptions for established/native forms (Annexes 2/4). Greek/Bulgarian serial conversions are positional, not phonetic. EU inserted article suffixes convert; national bis/ter usually remains (§23.15).
6. **Preserve quantities and financial distinctions.** ISO currency codes are compulsory with amounts in legal acts; symbols serve public/web contexts (§8.1, PDF 59). Do not blur EUR with EU or financial table scale with value. Percentage points differ from percentage change (49); kW differs from kWh and tonne from ton. Do not automatically convert non-SI quantities (§7.25). Commitments, payments, carryover, cancellation and decommitment carry different meanings (111). Preserve meaningful precision, deadlines versus durations, and entry into force versus application.
7. **Respect institutional, historical and publication scope.** EU is not Europe; Council, European Council and Council of Europe differ, as do CJEU, Court of Justice, General Court and ECtHR. Keep historical Community names and pre-2015 act numbering where appropriate. OJ references changed to act-by-act publication in October 2023 (108). Do not conflate transposition (directives) with implementation. Alphabetical versus protocol lists require different handling. Treaty signing and accession dates differ.
8. **Keep ISG separate.** The introduction (PDF 10) expressly says this guide does not replace the Interinstitutional Style Guide, Joint Practical Guide or other drafting resources. This run read the DGT PDF only, not ISG or the Country Compendium. DGT references to ISG are cross-references, not proof those external pages were read. OJ decimal/percent production conventions, legal numbering, bibliographies and protocol ordering often require separate ISG consultation.

## Source limitations and issues worth carrying forward

- **Internal Cyrillic conflict:** §9.5 (PDF 61) says hard/soft signs should be omitted. Annex 4 (PDF 131), visually checked, instead specifies Bulgarian ъ → a and ь → y and Belarusian soft-sign acute accents, alongside Russian/Ukrainian omissions. Use the language-specific mapping and flag the conflict rather than encoding a universal omission rule.
- **Extraction loses editorial markup:** PDF 79 contains struck-through possessive pronouns; visual inspection establishes that they are deletion examples, not recommended literal strings.
- **Historical/institutional facts are not independently verified current law.** For example, the protocol annex’s Pope Francis example is not current-officeholder evidence. Verify officeholders, organisational names, treaty cross-references, funding statuses and scientific nomenclature against primary current sources before operational reuse. A recent cover date does not guarantee every example was updated.
- **Layout verification:** rendered/inspected PDF 79, 124–125, 130–131 and 133 for strikeouts, table columns, merged cells, variants and article-suffix correspondence. All other pages were read in extracted text; no claim is made to visually inspect all 138 pages.
