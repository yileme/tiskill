## Proposed integration into existing practice

1. Put T into usage diagnosis/practice references: meaning shifts, argument structure, countability, collocation, institutional jargon and audience. Put C into plain-language planning/revision. A new standalone skill is not justified by these two books: their tasks fit existing usage/practice and clarity work.
2. Begin with purpose, audience and required reader action, then group messages and identify missing facts (C3–5). Review document structure before word substitution: useful summary, informative headings, logical links, tables/visuals where appropriate, coherent conclusion.
3. Recover the intended concept before choosing English. T9/16/17–18 expose unclear programme/budget hierarchies; T25 distinguishes management, checking and safeguards; T35 separates prediction, intention and legal provision; T55 requires the actual EU/EEA/Schengen boundary. C10 explicitly makes exact meaning a prerequisite to concreteness.
4. Keep concept evidence and wording evidence separate. Suggested record fields: intended concept and scope; authoritative domain/legal source; quoted/defined term status; candidate expression; grammar/collocation/reader evidence with source edition and page; context and audience; reason for retention/change; unresolved questions. This record is an integration recommendation inferred from the reading, not a procedure stated verbatim in either PDF.
5. Preserve consistent reference. C7 explicitly rejects swapping synonyms merely for stylistic variety and warns about one word naming two different things. Clarity does not mean simplifying every term or choosing a different short synonym on each occurrence.
6. Prefer direct verbs and visible agency where useful (C7–9), but do not guess an omitted agent. Maintain useful passives (C9), technical nouns, conditions, responsibility and modal force. T53's alternatives to shall are not interchangeable: must, may and will have distinct meanings.
7. Retain official/quoted terminology where necessary and explain it (T4,16,24–25,43,53,55–56; C12). Use short public-facing glosses as explanations, not replacement legal definitions. Do not silently narrow economic operator to company (T46) or enterprise to company (T30).
8. Diagnose false friends in context (C11; T10–12,16,27–31,35,41,47–48), distinguish grammar from register, and turn selected examples into meaning-preserving revision exercises. Prefer independently authored examples and rationales, not wholesale copied source lists.
9. Make abbreviations depend on frequency, reader familiarity and domain; clarify ambiguous expansions (C13). First-use definition/list may help, repeated expansions may obstruct.
10. Add substantive review beyond spellcheck: read as an outsider, check referents, logical conditions, agency, chronology, consistency and translation ambiguity; invite fresh-reader feedback (C14).

## Relationship to ISG and DGT

C15 itself routes publication conventions to ISG, English spelling/punctuation/usage to the DGT English Style Guide, legislative drafting to the Joint practical guide, and web writing to separate web guidance. Integrate these as distinct reference functions, with applicable current official instructions and legal definitions checked for the task. This run did not reread or verify current ISG/DGT editions or any external linked pages. Do not claim that either supplied PDF establishes a universal hierarchy over current institutional rules.

A concrete conflict is T12 footnote 18: Gardner deliberately uses UK/Irish number formatting rather than the ISG convention. Therefore do not import his formatting choices into an ISG-compliant output. His T28 recommendation to prefer perform/carry out over do also cannot become a plain-language default: C8 often removes light-verb phrases in favour of a direct verb.

## Limits that must survive integration

- T is a dated, opinionated UK/Irish-oriented usage guide (T2–5), not current universal English law or a binding legal terminology authority. It acknowledges change and specialist/internal exceptions. Its categorical statements about provide to (T49), decommit (T26), define (T27), actor/actorness (T9–10), and official terms require contemporary domain/usage checking before operational bans. No fresh corpus/dictionary verification was conducted here.
- T's EU quotations establish that an expression was used. Its cited dictionaries/BNC/native responses supply the author's usage argument. Neither establishes a universal conceptual definition; historical raw corpus counts are not normalised proof of unacceptability. T33's discussion of statistical MT reinforces that recycled institutional occurrences need not be independent endorsement.
- C explicitly calls its advice hints (C2). The 15-page guidepost and 20-word sentence average (C6) are not global constraints; short sentences must retain connective logic. Passive voice is allowed (C9), insider jargon can be suitable (C12), and common abbreviations need not be expanded mechanically (C13).
- C12's glosses are simplified and dated. For example, its candidate-country gloss should not be promoted into an exhaustive legal-status definition. T20's comitology abolition discussion must not be presented as verified current institutional law.
- Original references, examples, services and URLs are historical; links were not tested. Contact-sheet review checked layout, illustrations and table relationships; it is not a full-resolution typographic audit.
