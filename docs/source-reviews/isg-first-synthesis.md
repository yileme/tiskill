## High-value verified-in-PDF conclusions

1. **OJ is a specialised publication system.** Procedure determines legislative status; act labels alone do not (pp.18–28, 57–61). Part One is strict OJ editing, not universal law or general English prescription (p.6).
2. **Historical identifiers are evidence.** Preserve original act numbering/treaty names; October 2023 changes OJ citation from issue/page to document identification plus ELI. New L references require ELI; C ELI is recommended, not compulsory; earlier L identifiers are added if available (pp.21–23, 42–49, 60–61, 95–99).
3. **Stable structural references protect meaning.** Do not renumber inserted articles; distinguish explicit enumeration from ranges including inserted provisions (pp.48, 52, 95). General publications have more latitude in titles (p.95).
4. **Content structure precedes visual styling.** Consistent semantic headings and automatic notes support translation, navigation, contents and reliable production (pp.65–71). Camera-ready rules are a different workflow.
5. **Metadata does not establish rights.** ISBN/ISSN identify resources and confer no copyright status; medium/edition/version distinctions matter. DOI and ECLI serve different identification purposes (pp.72–78). Reuse and third-party rights vary (pp.84–89).
6. **Numbers and currencies need a context switch.** English ordinary publications use decimal points, OJ/multilingual material decimal commas; EUR 1 million is allowed generally but OJ uses full figures; whole-table unit is million EUR (pp.105, 109–110, 121–123).
7. **Naming/sorting rules are not interchangeable.** Member States follow source-language protocol order; mixed-country English prose sorts in English; shared multilingual tables may sort codes; language-version order differs from language-name order (pp.111–117).
8. **Reader-centred punctuation has room for judgement.** Serial comma when helpful, short introductory phrase commas optional, restrictive which permitted, and explicit rewrites preferable to subtle punctuation dependency (pp.150–152).
9. **Hyphenation is contextual.** Pre-noun compounds differ from predicative phrases; -ly combinations are normally unhyphenated; established spellings and EU-specific exceptions supersede mechanical prefix rules (p.153).
